# Cycle-aware training and online coaching — evidence and boundaries

Research checked 16 September 2026, against the brief supplied by the owner. Two of its central
citations were re-verified in this session; the rest are taken from the brief's own source list and
marked accordingly.

## 1. Cycle phase is a weak basis for prescribing training

- **McNulty et al., 2020** (*Sports Medicine* 50:1813–1827) — systematic review and meta-analysis of
  exercise performance in eumenorrheic women. Performance may be **trivially** reduced in the early
  follicular phase compared with other phases. Because the effect is trivial, between-study variation
  is large and many included studies were poor quality, the authors state that general guidelines
  cannot be formed and recommend a **personalised approach** instead.
  (verified — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32661839/)
- **Colenso-Semple et al., 2023** (*Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* 5:1054542) — umbrella
  review of reviews on resistance training. It concludes it is **premature** to claim that hormonal
  fluctuations across the cycle meaningfully affect acute strength performance or long-term strength
  and hypertrophy adaptations, and notes poor or absent verification of cycle phase in the underlying
  studies. (verified — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1054542/full)

**What this changed.** There is no four-phase training schedule anywhere in the product, and nothing
increases or decreases load because of a date. Suggestions come from what she logs today — energy,
cramps, appetite — and every suggested change states the symptom that produced it. A period by itself
never triggers a lighter workout.

## 2. Symptoms, pain and iron (from the brief's sources)

- PMS involves fatigue, bloating, sleep and appetite changes that differ between people and between
  months; symptom diaries, balanced eating, exercise and sleep are the standard advice
  (NHS: premenstrual syndrome). → Short optional check-ins, flexible meal choices, personal history,
  and **no fixed phase-based calorie or macro changes**.
- Gentle movement helps some people with period pain, and pain that disrupts life needs medical
  attention (NHS: period pain). → Optional mobility or walking for manageable discomfort, and a
  separate support state that **replaces** the training suggestion when symptoms are severe.
- Heavy periods raise the risk of iron deficiency; lentils and beans provide iron and vitamin C helps
  absorb plant iron (NIH: iron). → Ordinary iron-containing meals appear as options with the reason
  stated. The app never diagnoses low iron from tiredness and never recommends supplements.
- Cycle length varies, calendar dates cannot confirm ovulation, and some hormonal contraception
  suppresses it (NHS: menstrual cycle). → Logged dates and estimates look different, symptom-only
  mode exists, and **no day is ever labelled confirmed ovulation**.

## 3. The medical boundaries the product keeps

1. No "medically verified" badge, no hormone chart, no readiness score.
2. Dates are estimates, stated as a range with the reason; past estimates never become logged periods.
3. Irregular cycles, hormonal contraception and perimenopause switch forecasting **off**, not to a guess.
4. Pregnancy or recent postpartum pauses cycle-based adaptation and says so, without implying the
   standard plan is suitable.
5. Severe or worsening pain gets medical guidance and a route to care — never a meal or a workout.
6. Cycle inputs never reach analytics, feeds, ads or sponsors, and can be deleted on their own.
7. Food is never described as a cure for cramps or a hormone treatment.

## 4. Online coaching: product patterns, not health claims

The brief's coaching references (Everfit's training, nutrition, forms and communication pages, and
ABC Trainerize's feature list) describe what coaching platforms commonly provide: assigned programs
from a library, 30-day meal scheduling with reusable days, intake forms and recurring check-ins,
direct messages with voice notes, and one shared record across coach and client. They establish
**common product patterns, not evidence of better outcomes**, and the schedule and rules in this
mockup are design choices.

**What this changed.**

- One plan, one set of logs. Coaching does not create a second food diary or a second workout history.
- The client's day shows the coach's assignment first and labels it *From your coach*; if no session
  is scheduled, the app says so instead of inventing one.
- Prescribed values and actual results are separate everywhere. A prescription is never counted as
  completed work.
- A coach's message never changes a plan by itself: publishing does, and the published change appears
  as a labelled card in both views.
- Revisions default to future dates. A session already running, and anything already logged, stay on
  the version she used.
- Nutrition totals appear only where verified ingredient data exists; otherwise the screen says
  **nutrition details unavailable** rather than calculating a plausible number.
- Meal planning shows allergies while editing, and an incompatible ingredient blocks assignment.

## 5. Where cycle support meets coaching

- The same daily check-in serves both. She is never asked for energy or appetite twice.
- In coached mode, "your planned workout" means the coach's assignment for today.
- The coach can attach an easier session in advance, labelled **Coach-approved option**; everything
  else the app offers is labelled **App suggestion**.
- She can take the easier option or rest immediately. **Coach approval is never required to stop
  exercising.** The original assignment stays visible beside what she actually did.
- Cycle sharing is three independent switches, off by default, with a preview before saving. With
  sharing off the coach sees "client selected a lighter session" and the panel reads **Not shared** —
  never "no symptoms". Ending the coaching relationship switches ongoing sharing off.

## 6. What this mockup does not contain

No backend, no prediction engine, no health-platform integrations, no real notifications, no payments
inside the coaching flow, no fertility or pregnancy programming, no supplements, and no medical
chatbot. Records are fictional and in memory, which is stated wherever a state could be mistaken for a
live service.
