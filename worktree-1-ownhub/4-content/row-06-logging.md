Row 06
Logging, rebuilt — confirm instead of enter
One interaction model replaces every form in the product: the app states what it thinks happened, she taps once to agree. Editing exists, but it is the exception path, never the route.
What the research says, and what I did about it
Passive logging backfires
Wearable and photo-based tracking produced weaker habit formation than a plain database app — people forgot to use them. Effortless is not the same as memorable.
So: keep one deliberate tap, and anchor it to a cue she already has — opening the app, and finishing a set.
Consistency beats completeness
Frequent tracking predicted results. Complete tracking — every food, every day — did not.
So: a half-logged day is a logged day. No screen ever asks her to finish filling something in.
Daily streaks cause the dropout
All-or-nothing counters lose people right after the first break. A weekly target keeps the pull without the cliff.
So: the unit of consistency is a week, not a day, and one quiet week in eight is free and automatic.
Follow-up questions are friction
Clarifying prompts after a log were rated relevant and still made logging feel harder, especially with generic defaults.
So: nothing is ever asked after a log. The app assumes, logs, and shows an undo.
Search is the friction
The cost is decisions, not seconds: thirty near-identical results, then a variant, then a serving size.
So: she logs plates she has eaten before, not foods. Search is the fallback, and it answers with her own history first.
Gamification is a bridge
The effect on activity is modest and fades after roughly six months. It buys the early weeks, not the year.
So: points reward only behaviours that move her goal, they cap daily, and no points exist for opening the app.

What I deliberately left out
✕
A daily streak counter. It is the single mechanic most likely to make her stop opening the app.
✕
Photo calorie scanning. Out of scope in your brief, and the evidence says it weakens the habit anyway.
✕
Weight-based leaderboards. Challenges rank by sessions completed, so a 3 kg dumbbell and a 30 kg barbell score the same.
✕
Badges for trivia. No reward for filling in a profile, inviting friends, or reading a screen.
✕
A new tab. Progression lives on Today and inside Progress. The five-tab nav is unchanged.
