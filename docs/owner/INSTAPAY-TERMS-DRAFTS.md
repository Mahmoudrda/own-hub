## A. Client terms (clauses now in Terms of Use, sections 2, 3, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15 and 17)

- **A1. No money held.** Own Hub never holds, routes, initiates or accesses the money between a member and her coach. The member pays the coach's own InstaPay address from her own bank app. Own Hub is not a bank or a payment provider. Separately, the coach pays Own Hub's own monthly fee to Own Hub by InstaPay (B4).
- **A2. What Own Hub does.** It shows who to pay and how much, records the payment, asks the coach to confirm that it arrived, and, if there is a dispute, a person at Own Hub decides it.
- **A3. Check the name.** The member is told to check that the name her bank shows is her coach's before she sends. Own Hub cannot undo an InstaPay transfer. A transfer to a wrong account is for the member's bank; Own Hub does not decide such cases.
- **A4. No check can prove a payment; false proofs.** Proof is the member's transaction number and optional bank screenshots. Sending a false proof breaches the Terms. Code: staff decide, with a written reason, that a proof was false (`resolved_unpaid` with `proofFalse`); that records a strike (`paymentStrikes`, reason `false_proof`). Two live strikes inside 90 days set `clientSuspended` at once (`strikes._standing_changes`): the member cannot start a new InstaPay payment (`create`) and cannot send proof for one that is waiting (`proof.submit`). She is sent a notification afterwards (`instapayPaused`, only if she allowed payment alerts). There is no notice and no step to reply before it takes effect. The pause is undone only if a different member of staff reopens the decision (`strikes.reopen` reverses the strikes and lifts a pause that came from them). Fraud may be reported to the authorities and the evidence kept for that. `[OWNER: no appeal path exists]` `[COUNSEL: no appeal route and no reply step before a payment pause]` The site says both plainly and offers only the support mailbox.
- **A5. Access before the coach confirms.** Short access may be given while a proof is checked: at most 7 days, or the length of the package if that is shorter (`coach.grant_provisional`). A green proof gives it at once, an amber one after 24 hours with no objection (`timers.grant_amber`). It ends when the short access runs out if the payment is still not confirmed (`timers.lapse_access` and the hourly subscription sweeper); a later paid decision restarts it only if the coaching can still be opened. The coach's fee is owed from the moment short access starts (`fees.at_activation`).
- **A6. Refund.** `[COUNSEL: refund floor under Law 181/2018 vs current behaviour]`
  Code facts (`payments/refund.py`, `payments/staff.py`, `coaching/refunds.py` is the older free-coaching flow):
  - A member can ask once per payment (`REFUND_ALREADY_REQUESTED`), only while the payment is `confirmed` or `resolved_paid`, and only within 14 days of `refund.started_at`: the coach's confirmation, else the staff decision, else the last status change. The 14 days do not run from the payment itself or from the member's request to pay.
  - The coach has 3 days. She can send the money back by InstaPay and record it (`refund.record`: transaction number, amount, time; Own Hub cannot see the transfer, so the record is her word), or she can deny (`refund.deny`, reason `sessions_delivered`, `outside_terms` or `other`). A coach who stays silent for 3 days sends the request to staff (`ESCALATE_STATES`).
  - After a denial the member can ask staff (`open_service_dispute`, kind `refund`); after a recorded refund she can say it never arrived (kind `refund_not_received`). Staff decide with a written reason. Their recommendation (`staff.recommend`, never binding) is `refund_owed` only if the package included sessions and none were used; otherwise `no_refund`. If staff order a refund the coach must record at least the amount they named; nothing in code enforces the due date (`refundDueAt`, 7 days, is written and never read).
  - Own Hub moves no refund money. The coach sends it back.
  - The published text now describes exactly this, says the coach is the seller of the coaching and sends the refund, and says that nothing in the Terms reduces a right the Consumer Protection Law gives (Terms 15, already there).
  Law 181/2018 point for counsel: for a distance contract the Consumer Protection Law gives a 14-day withdrawal and a refund within 7 days, and rights cannot be waived. Current behaviour is narrower: a coach may deny, and staff recommend a refund only for an unused package.
  Options: (a) keep the behaviour and the plain text; (b) change the code so a request inside 14 days of payment (not of confirmation) is refunded unless the service is fully used, with staff only deciding "fully used"; (c) other, for example a different split between coach and Own Hub, or telling the member in the app that her statutory right is unaffected.
- **A7. Service problem.** If the coach does not deliver, the member can open a service problem (once per payment for that kind, until 14 days after the package ended, at most 3 disputes a month: `disputesPerMonth`). Code: what Own Hub gathers is `serviceFacts` (sessions used and included) and the member's own note (`staff/dispute`, readable only by staff). Own Hub does not read the coaching chat for this; staff read a thread only after a report (Privacy 3.16). Staff decide `refund_owed` (with an amount) or `no_refund`. A service decision does not record a strike: `strikes.after_decision` records strikes only for a not-received dispute (coach) or a false proof (member).
- **A8. Record.** The payment record a member can download is not a tax invoice (stated in Terms 9 and Coach Terms 9).
- **A9. Who is who.** `[COUNSEL: the coach as seller and Own Hub's position]` Own Hub is not a bank or a payment provider, is not a party to the coaching agreement between the member and her coach, does not promise results and does not give medical advice. The coach is an independent supplier, responsible for her own service and for refunds. The Terms say this in section 9 (and keep section 4 on medical advice and section 13 on results).
- **A10. What the labels mean.** "Verified coach" and "Certificates checked": a woman reviewer at Own Hub approved the coach's application, and for each certificate she ticked she opened the file and judged it readable and the coach's own (`applications.review`: approval needs at least one ticked certificate). There is no check with the body that issued a certificate. "Checked by Own Hub" on a recipe means Own Hub's automated check of the recipe's allergen list (`Recipe.autoChecked`), not a medical review. The Terms say exactly this and no more.

## B. Coach terms (clauses now in Coach Terms, sections 5, 6 and 9)

- **B1. Paid directly.** A member pays the coach's InstaPay address. The coach keeps the address and account name exactly as her bank shows them. A new address takes effect after 24 hours and needs a recent sign-in (a code sent to her phone: the only sign-in is a phone code; `address.set_address` requires `recent_sign_in`); members with a payment waiting are told.
- **B2. Confirm honestly.** The coach checks her own bank for the exact amount and transaction number, then confirms, or says it has not arrived and attaches her bank's list for that day. A reminder goes at 48 hours. At 72 hours without an answer the payment goes to Own Hub staff. A member whose proof passed the automatic checks may already have short access (A5).
- **B3. Strikes and suspension (as built).** `[OWNER: no appeal path exists]` `[COUNSEL: no appeal route and no reply step before a payment pause]`
  - A claim of non-payment that her own bank list contradicts (the exact amount or the transaction number is on it) is refused on the spot (`claims.not_received`: `CLAIM_CONTRADICTED`, `autoRejectedClaims` +1). It is not a strike.
  - A coach strike comes only when staff resolve a not-received dispute as `resolved_paid` (`reason` `lost_dispute`), with a written reason.
  - Two live strikes inside 90 days flag the coach (`coachFlagged`, `flagReason` `twoStrikes`). A dispute rate also flags her: at least `disputeRateMinPayments` (10) confirmed payments and `disputeRatePercent` (10) percent or more lost (`strikes.rate_flag`; both owner-tunable in `coachingConfig/instapay`).
  - A flagged coach is reviewed by two different members of staff (`APPROVALS_NEEDED = 2`, within 7 days, each with a reason of at least 10 characters). Only then are new payments suspended (`strikes.set_coach_status`). One member of staff can lift it.
  - A suspension stops new payments. It does not by itself end coachings that are already running.
  - No step lets her reply before the flag, and there is no appeal route.
- **B4. The fee (as built).** `fees.at_activation`, `fees.recompute`, `fees.generate`, `fees.mark_overdue`.
  - The rate is read from `coachingConfig/instapay` when the payment's access starts, then kept on that payment (`feeBps`). Defaults: 15 percent (1500) for a client who found her in the app, 8 percent (800) for a client she brought with her own invite code. The owner can change them. If she does, change the Coach Terms before the change takes effect.
  - The base is `feeBasePiasters`, the package price, not the few extra piasters. The fee is rounded down.
  - It is lowered if she accepts a lower amount in writing (at most 20 percent lower, `acceptLowerMinPercent` 80) and after a refund (the base becomes what she kept). It is waived if a payment is found unpaid.
  - It is owed from the moment access starts, which can be short access before she confirms. It is not tied to her tap on "confirm".
  - A monthly statement is made for the previous Cairo calendar month. `dueAt` is 30 days after it is made (`feeOverdueDays`). A fee on a payment in review is listed as pending. She pays Own Hub by InstaPay and records the transfer number; the owner confirms it; nothing is debited automatically.
  - A statement unpaid 30 days after it was made stops new clients. Clients already coached are not touched. `fees.record_payment` and `fees.confirm_payment`.
  - Own Hub keeps the fee statements for the period in D1.
- **B5. Refunds.** As A6. `[OWNER: ignoring a staff refund decision is not enforced by code]` The earlier text said that ignoring a staff refund decision ends her ability to take payments. Nothing reads the 7-day `refundDueAt`. The site now says that staff may suspend her ability to take payments (two staff, B3), which is the only lever that exists.
- **B6. Stay on Own Hub (D64).** While she coaches a client she met through Own Hub, and for 12 months after the last coaching on Own Hub ends, she may not coach her or take payment from her outside Own Hub. It does not apply to clients she brought with her own invite code. The site says this is a duty and that Own Hub may warn her, charge 3 months of Own Hub's share (a fair estimate of the lost fee, not a punishment) and, for a second breach, suspend her. `[COUNSEL: the 3-month charge and the 12-month rule are not enforced by code]` No code tracks the 12 months or raises the charge; staff would act by hand, and a suspension is possible only through the two-staff `set_coach_status` (B3). The general ban in Terms 8 and Coach Terms 5 now forbids moving a client away from Own Hub to avoid the platform's rules or fee (a payment made through the app's InstaPay steps is not that), and Coach Terms 5 says an invite-code client is outside the 12-month rule, as section 9 already does.
- **B7. Tax.** She is responsible for her own tax status and for any invoice she must issue.
- **B8. Records and reporting.** Payment records and dispute evidence are kept (D). Fraud may be reported to the authorities.
- **Price note.** The price she sets is the price the member pays, plus a few extra piasters (0.01 to 0.99) that make the amount match only that payment. Those piasters go to the coach and are shown to the member ("pay exactly 500.37").

## C. Dispute process (one paragraph for the Terms or a help page, if counsel wants it written out)

When a member sends proof, the coach is asked to confirm. A reminder goes at 48 hours. At 72 hours without an answer, or at once if the coach says it has not arrived (unless her own bank list shows the payment, which refuses her claim on the spot), the payment goes to Own Hub staff, who aim to decide within two working days (owner target). A red check result also sends a payment to staff at once. Staff look at, in order: the unique amount and reference; the transaction number; what the screenshots show and how well they match; flags such as a reused number or picture; the timeline; each party's dispute history; and, if still unclear, a bank statement line from both parties with other lines hidden. The decision is recorded with a written reason. A decision to suspend a coach needs two staff. Only a woman reviewer (the `womanReviewer` claim, no admin) can open or decide a payment dispute (`staff_list_payment_queue` and the other staff payment functions).

## D. Retention (owner decision 12.4; the site states these numbers)

1. **Payment records, fee statements, strike records.** Code: `retention.RECORD_RETENTION_YEARS = 5`, marked UNVERIFIED with counsel, and "nothing deletes the record in this release". On account deletion the record stays with her uid replaced by a random token, her typed names emptied, a coach's address snapshot emptied, and the free text she or the coach wrote deleted (`deletion._pseudonymise_instapay_payments`). The site says: kept for 5 years, with no automatic deletion yet when the 5 years end. `[OWNER: deletion job after 5 years not built]` `[COUNSEL: a 5-year period for payment records, fee statements and strike records; what must happen after]`
2. **Payment screenshots.** `retention.sweep_screenshots` deletes the pictures 90 days after a payment reaches `confirmed`, `resolved_paid`, `resolved_unpaid`, `refunded` or `cancelled` (measured from `statusAt`, so a closed dispute starts the 90 days when its decision moves the status). A screenshot is deleted at once with the account. `[OWNER: refund_denied screenshots are never swept]` A payment that stays `refund_denied` is not in `FINISHED`, so its pictures stay until the account is deleted. The site says so.
3. **Consent records.** Code: `users/{uid}/consents/*` sits under the user document, which `deletion` deletes with `recursive_delete`. So a consent record is kept while the account exists and deleted with it. The site now says that (Privacy 3.2 and the delete page). `[COUNSEL: consent records are deleted with the account, the Executive Regulations ask for a record]`: if counsel wants a consent log kept after deletion, it is a code change plus the deletion page and Privacy 6.
4. **What stays after deletion that the site did not name.** `[OWNER: staff review data survives account deletion; strikes and staff data are not in the data export]` `payments/{id}/staff/review` (the receipt check results, picture hashes, the transaction number the checks read, and the phone's reading: booleans, the transaction number, one different amount and the sender handle) is not deleted by `deletion` (only `staff/dispute`, `staff/evidence` and `staff/statement` are). The registries `paymentTxnNumbers` and `paymentImageHashes` carry no uid and stay. The timeline `events` stay. `functions/tools/export_user.py` exports the payment documents, the payment profile and standing, and fee statements; it does not export `paymentStrikes` or the staff documents. The privacy page now names what persists. Adding `paymentStrikes` to the export is small, but needs a test; staff review data should stay out of the export (counsel to decide).
5. **Not stored:** the text Google Cloud Vision reads from a picture is held in memory for one request (`ocr.TextReader`) and never stored. The server keeps the result of each check, the transaction number and the picture fingerprints.

## I. Gaps the site must never fill with an invention

The site shows the publisher as an individual, Mahmoud Reda, Egypt, with a mailbox. The code does not say which service it is. The site says "a team chat service, which may be outside Egypt".
