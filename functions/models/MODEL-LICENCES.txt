Bundled model weights — licences, provenance, and why these two and not the
obvious ones.

Checked on 2026-09-25 against the upstream repositories. Re-check before any
model is swapped: a permissive code licence on a repository does NOT imply a
permissive licence on the weights inside it, and this is precisely where face
recognition projects trip people up.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAVEAT on the published accuracy figure — read this before quoting it
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.9940 is a verification accuracy on a benchmark of ordinary photographs of the
same person. That is NOT the task this function performs.

Our task is card-portrait-to-live-selfie:
  * the card portrait is a laminated, sub-300-DPI print of a photograph that may
    be up to seven years old (Egyptian national ID cards are valid for seven
    years), re-photographed by a phone through a reflective surface;
  * the selfie is a live capture under whatever light is in her room;
  * for a large share of our users the card portrait shows a hijab and the
    selfie may not, or the other way round.

Every one of those shifts the similarity distribution downward relative to the
benchmark. The published number therefore sets an optimistic ceiling and gives
us no usable false-accept or false-reject rate for our own population. The
operating point in identity/config.py is set conservatively for that reason,
with an explicit band routed to a human rather than guessed at. See the
reasoning recorded there.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Models deliberately NOT used
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
InsightFace / ArcFace pretrained packs (buffalo_l and friends)
    Higher accuracy than SFace, and unusable here. The InsightFace project
    states that its pretrained models are available for non-commercial research
    purposes only. A paid product gating paid accounts is commercial use. The
    accuracy would have been nice; the licence is not negotiable, and "nobody
    checks" is not a licensing strategy for a company that will one day be
    audited.

dlib_face_recognition_resnet_model_v1
    dlib's own code is Boost Software License, which is fine. The recognition
    weights were trained on a scrape that includes datasets with research-only
    terms, and dlib's model repository does not carry a clean, unambiguous
    commercial grant covering that file. Unresolved provenance is the same
    problem as a bad licence, so it was left alone.

FaceNet ports (davidsandberg / facenet-pytorch)
    MIT code, but the distributed checkpoints are trained on VGGFace2 and
    CASIA-WebFace, whose own terms are research-oriented. Same unresolved
    provenance question as dlib.

A hosted face-match API (AWS Rekognition, Azure Face, Google Cloud Vision's
face features)
    Cost aside, sending her face to a third party is the exact thing this
    design exists to avoid. Vision is used here for text only.
