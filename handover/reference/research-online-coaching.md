## 2. Workflow-by-workflow: what the best tools offer, and tap targets

Taps are EST for a coach with a laptop or phone, after the first-time setup. "Target" is what Own Hub should hit, not what competitors achieve.

### 2.1 Build a workout
Common pattern: search library, tap to add, set sets/reps/weight fields, add note, attach or inherit demo video, save as template.
- Library search: Trainerize, TrueCoach, My PT Hub all ship 3,000 to 8,000+ demos. Own videos can be uploaded in TrueCoach and My PT Hub.
- Supersets: Kahunas states them; My PT Hub and TrueCoach pages do not (TrueCoach says "circuits" only).
- Rest timer: Kahunas states it.
- Notes per exercise: My PT Hub, TrueCoach ("custom instructions").
- Target for Own Hub: add one exercise in 2 taps (search, tap result); defaults prefilled from the last time that exercise was used; a 6-exercise workout in under 15 taps; duplicate-a-workout in 2 taps.

### 2.2 Build a multi-week programme
- Trainerize: phased programs, or copy and adjust a template.
- My PT Hub: multi-week programs, plus "progressive programs that adjust to client performance".
- Everfit and PT Distinction: programs, plus group programs (one program, many clients).
- Copy week is not stated on any vendor page I read, but is a standard builder action; treat as expected, not proven.
- Target: "repeat last week" 1 tap; "add 2.5 kg / +1 rep to all" 2 taps; a 4-week block from a template in under 10 taps.

### 2.3 Assign to a client
- TrueCoach: apply templates to several clients; reviewers complain updating a program means doing it client by client (SECONDARY).
- Everfit and PT Distinction: group programs avoid that.
- Target: assign from client page 3 taps (pick programme, pick start date, confirm); client gets a push and sees "Today" with one Start button.

### 2.4 Meal plans, recipes, swaps, grocery list
- My PT Hub: macro calculator, custom meals and recipes, 650k food database.
- Eat This Much: auto plan from calories/macros, pantry, grocery list that follows meal changes. Best on swaps and grocery list.
- Trainerize: Smart and Flexible planners. TrueCoach: meal plans only as documents (weak).
- Target: coach picks a plan template, sets kcal and protein, client sees a week; swap a meal in 2 taps from a coach-approved list; grocery list generated in 1 tap. Recipes live in the app (Own Hub already has 301 meals).

### 2.5 Messaging
- Voice notes: Trainerize, Everfit, Kahunas (base tier). Not named for TrueCoach.
- Photo/video: TrueCoach (pictures, video, GIFs).
- Scheduled and broadcast: Everfit, My PT Hub, PT Distinction.
- Templates/quick replies: no vendor page names them; only a DM-script seller (weak). Treat as a gap Own Hub can win.
- Read receipts: not found on any page read.
- Target: reply with a saved quick reply in 2 taps; record a voice note by hold-to-record in 1 gesture; form-check video from client opens in the thread with a "reply on video" action.

### 2.6 Check-ins, photos, measurements
- Kahunas: custom weekly forms, side-by-side comparison, wearable sync.
- My PT Hub: automated check-ins and forms (incl. waivers).
- Everfit: forms plus in-app prompts.
- Target: client completes a weekly check-in in under 60 seconds (5 or fewer questions, 1-tap scales, optional photo); coach reviews all check-ins in one list, replies in 2 taps.

### 2.7 Coach profile / portfolio page
- Tools above sell branded apps and storefronts (PT Distinction, Fitr, Kahunas top tier). No page read described a coach profile page inside the client app.
- Target: one screen: photo, short voice-or-video intro, credentials, who she works with, plans offered, one "Message Rahma" button. (Own Hub already has coach_portfolio_view.)

### 2.8 Onboarding intake (PAR-Q, goals, injuries)
- My PT Hub: forms and waivers. Automated onboarding flows: Everfit, My PT Hub, PT Distinction.
- PAR-Q+ (2023) opens with 7 yes/no questions; any "yes" goes to follow-up pages; follow-up may route to a qualified professional or physician; clearance is valid at most 12 months (SECONDARY summary).
- Target: intake in under 3 minutes, one question per screen, auto-flag for the coach when any health "yes".

### 2.9 Scheduling
- Trainerize: 1:1 and class booking with calendar sync. Practice Better: client self-booking.
- Target day one: not needed beyond "book a call" link; skip a calendar.

### 2.10 Habit tracking
- Trainerize (streaks, badges), TrueCoach (daily/weekly/monthly), Kahunas (custom daily forms), Fitr (daily).
- Target: 1 to 3 habits max, each a single tap per day.

### 2.11 Compliance dashboard
- TrueCoach: automated risk alerts when clients fall off; filter by program due date. Trainerize/Everfit: progress reports and insights.
- Target: one list sorted by "needs attention" (missed 2+ sessions, no check-in, red-flag answer, unread message), each row 1 tap to reply.

### 2.12 Reminders / automation
- Everfit, My PT Hub, PT Distinction, Trainerize: scheduled and auto messages, onboarding sequences. SECONDARY (Everfit blog, HubFit): a missed-session follow-up and an escalation when a client goes silent.

### 2.13 Business side
- Stripe recurring and one-off is the norm (Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit, Kahunas). TrueCoach payments only in US/UK/CA/AU. My PT Hub says 190+ countries.
- Packages: PT Distinction and My PT Hub sell packages via link or site.
- Own Hub is free with payments off for now; keep packages out of day one.

## 3. Workflows coaches say save the most time (mostly vendor/secondary claims)
1. Reusable templates for workouts and programs, then copy and tweak (most-cited; "halves build time" is a vendor claim).
2. Automated weekly check-in prompts plus follow-up after a missed session.
3. Automated onboarding: intake form, welcome message, first workout delivered without manual steps.
4. Group/broadcast programs so one edit reaches many clients (Everfit, PT Distinction).
5. Batching: review all check-ins in one place, answer messages in set blocks.
6. Pre-built on-demand libraries (HubFit blog).
7. Voice notes and short video replies instead of long typing (no source measuring this; plausible, unproven).
8. AI first-draft of workouts or meal plans that the coach then edits (Trainerize, Everfit, PT Distinction). Reviewer note: works for standard plans, weak on complex progressions (SECONDARY).

## 4. Top complaints about these tools
- Slow or clunky app; outages that lock out coaches and clients (Trainerize, per a competitor's blog, so treat as biased).
- Bulk edits are per client (TrueCoach, Capterra reviewers).
- Builders either too simple for multi-phase programming (TrueCoach, per FitBudd, a competitor) or with a learning curve (Everfit advanced features, Cronometer Pro grouped foods).
- Automation and add-ons cost extra (Everfit reviewer; Kahunas group messaging and automation are higher tiers; branded app needs top tier plus Apple fee).
- Nutrition is thin in some tools (TrueCoach meal plans as documents only).
- Regional gaps: payments limited by country (TrueCoach).
- Caveat: no source measured clicks or time per workout. Most review pages are old or written by competitors.

## 5. What clients value (vendor opinion, not research)
- A visible plan: today's workout on the first screen.
- One tap to start; logging that is fast enough to do between sets (vendor claim: 45% higher retention for clients who log in-app, no method given).
- Feedback loops: a reply to each check-in, ideally personal. "Clients who feel heard and guided are more likely to stick" is a vendor claim.
- Predictable weekly rhythm and small clear commitments, done or missed.
- First two weeks decide retention (FitBudd claim): plan delivered, then contact, not silence.
- Feeling seen: no measured source. Own Hub design inference: use the client's name and her stated goal in replies, acknowledge a missed week without guilt.

## 6. Safety and ethics norms (secondary sources; verify with a clinician before shipping)
- Scope of practice: a trainer or nutrition coach refers to a registered dietitian or physician for disease states affected by nutrition (diabetes, heart disease, GI disease, eating disorders, osteoporosis, high cholesterol). Medical nutrition therapy belongs to licensed dietitians (NSCA text excerpt).
- Eating disorders: coaches watch for warning signs and refer; they do not try to treat. A UK trainer-education site says do not prescribe calories, macros, meal plans, shopping lists or strict rules where red flags exist (NASM study-guide wording agrees on referral).
- Health screening: PAR-Q+ 2023 style yes/no screen; any "yes" means follow-up, a professional conversation or medical clearance; re-screen at most every 12 months or when health changes.
- Pregnancy and postpartum (ACOG summaries via DONA and Lamaze; not ACOG's own text): about 150 minutes a week of moderate activity is typical if no complications; moderate means "somewhat hard" (Borg 13 to 14); stop and seek care for vaginal bleeding, regular painful contractions, fluid leakage, dizziness, headache, chest pain, muscle weakness affecting balance, calf pain or swelling. Postpartum return needs clearance; no detailed postpartum rules were verified here.
- Cycle: no authoritative source read in this pass. Treat cycle-based programming as optional, never a prescription, until a source is found.
- Minors, injuries, medication: not sourced here. Keep a "see your doctor" gate for any health flag.
- Egypt-specific rules on coaching scope or data: not researched.

## 7. Minimum day-one feature set for one coach (Rahma), with "few clicks" targets

Must have (all aimed at Rahma's time):
1. Exercise library with search, and her own video per exercise. Add exercise: 2 taps.
2. Workout builder: sets, reps, rest, note, superset flag. Duplicate workout 2 taps. Save as template 1 tap.
3. Programme builder: weeks x days grid, "repeat last week" 1 tap, template library (start with her 3 plans).
4. Assign: from client page, 3 taps (programme, start date, confirm).
5. Client "Today" screen: plan visible, one Start button, log in the flow, finish screen with a message from Rahma.
6. Messaging: text, voice note (hold to record), photo and short video form-check, saved quick replies (2 taps), read receipt. Quick replies in EN and Egyptian Arabic.
7. Weekly check-in: 5 questions or fewer, 1-tap scales, optional photo, under 60 seconds for the client; Rahma reviews in one list, replies in 2 taps.
8. Intake with PAR-Q+ style screen, goals, injuries, pregnancy/postpartum, eating history: under 3 minutes; any health "yes" raises a flag on Rahma's list and limits what plans can be assigned until she reviews.
9. Compliance list ("needs attention"): missed 2+ sessions, no check-in, health flag, unread message; 1 tap to reply.
10. Reminders: workout day push, check-in day push, one follow-up after a missed week (kind tone, no guilt).
11. Meal plan: template with kcal and protein target, swap from approved list in 2 taps, grocery list 1 tap. Gate behind the eating-disorder screen: no calorie or macro targets for flagged clients.
12. Coach page: photo, intro video or voice, credentials, one Message button.

Should have soon: progress photos and measurements (client-controlled, private), 1 to 3 habits, broadcast message, group programme assignment (edit once, many clients).
Skip on day one: scheduling calendar, packages and payments, community forum, branded storefront, wearable sync, AI plan generation (use Rahma's hand-built templates; add AI only as a draft she edits).

Safety rules to build in (mapped from section 6): referral message and "see your doctor" screen; pregnancy/postpartum track with stop-signs shown inside workouts; no weight-loss targets or very low calorie targets; Rahma sees every flag before the first plan goes out.

Where Own Hub can do better than the field:
- Arabic and RTL: none of the tools read mention it.
- Women-only context: pregnancy, postpartum and cycle handled with sourced caution (none of the tools read mention them).
- Quick replies and read receipts: named by no vendor page.
- Fewer screens: competitors sell breadth; a solo coach needs a short list.
