The short version#

Own Hub collects nothing about you. Everything you put into it stays on your phone.

There is no account to make, no server to send anything to, and no company on the other end receiving your training, your meals or your cycle. The app writes what you log onto your own handset so that it is still there tomorrow, and one button in your profile erases all of it.

That is not a promise about how carefully we handle your data. It is a statement about software that has no way to send it anywhere, and the rest of this page explains why you can check that for yourself.

2. How to read this policy#

  • Part A is this release, the version you can install today. It is complete on its own.
  • Part B describes the release that adds accounts and a server. None of it is running. It cannot start without a new version of the app, a new version of this policy, and your consent where consent is required. It is published early so you can read it before you decide to make an account.

If you are reading this from a store listing, only Part A describes software you can install.

Part A, the version on the store#

A1. What it collects: nothing#

Own Hub has no server. It cannot send anything anywhere.

That is a statement about the software, and it is checkable:

  • The app’s whole dependency list is flutter, flutter_localizations, intl, meta, crypto, shared_preferences, url_launcher and cupertino_icons. There is no network library of any kind in it. No Firebase, no analytics library, no advertising library, no crash-reporting library, no HTTP client.
  • On Android, the released app does not even ask for permission to use the internet.
  • We receive nothing about you. Not your name, not your phone number, not what you log, not which screens you open, not whether you opened the app at all. We could not tell you how many people are using it beyond what Apple and Google count for their own stores.
  • There is no advertising and no tracking. The app never reads an advertising identifier, never asks permission to track you, and shares nothing with ad networks or data brokers.
  • There is no account. Nothing in the app identifies you, because the app was never told who you are.

A2. What it keeps on your phone, and where#

Everything you log is written to your own handset, so that closing the app does not lose your week.

Where
The phone’s own private settings store, inside the app’s sandbox. On iOS that is the app’s user defaults; on Android it is the app’s private preferences file. No other app can read it.
What
One document, holding your training, your meals, your weekly goal, your personal bests, your measurements, your cycle days, symptoms and settings, your privacy switches, what you told us you avoid eating, your setup answers, and your language.
When
A few hundred milliseconds after you change something, and again when you swipe the app away.
Who can read it
This app, on this phone. Nothing uploads it, nothing backs it up to us, and there is no analytics library in the app that could read it.
How to erase it
Profile, then Your data. See A5.

This is still not collection. Both Apple and Google define collecting as data leaving the device. Nothing here leaves it.

A3. The features that look like they need a server, and what they actually do#

Signing up or signing in
There is none. The app opens straight onto your own Home. No number is asked for, no code is sent, and there is no password anywhere in the product.
Identity checks, ID and selfie
Not in this version. There is no camera in the app, and the screens that would use one are switched off.
A community, posts, comments, groups
Not in this version. There is nothing to post to and nobody to post to. No part of the app connects you to another person.
Coaching, a coach, payments
Not in this version. Nothing in the app can be bought, and no price is shown anywhere.
An assistant
Not in this version. No question can be asked, nothing you write is sent to any AI service, and the app contains no key that could reach one.
Photos, video, voice notes
Cannot be created. There is no camera, no gallery access, no microphone and no speech recognition.
Opening our privacy policy, terms or support page
These three rows hand a fixed address, ours, to your phone’s own browser. The app has no browser of its own, it fetches nothing, and it learns nothing about what you do once the page opens.

A4. Permissions it asks for#

None. No camera, no photos, no microphone, no contacts, no location, no calendar, no health data, no notifications, no motion or fitness sensors. The iOS build contains no permission text at all, because nothing in it requests a permission.

The app is not connected to Apple Health, Google Fit, Health Connect, a step counter, or any sensor. Everything the app knows about your training is what you typed into it.

A5. Deleting everything#

Profile, then Your data. A sheet tells you what deleting removes, that it happens at once and that none of it can be brought back. The button under it does exactly that.

It erases the document described in A2, and empties everything the app is holding: your training, your meals, your measurements, your cycle, your settings and your answers. The app goes back to its first day and says so.

Two things worth knowing:

  1. There is nothing on our side to delete, because we never had anything. You do not have to ask us, write to us or wait for us.
  2. Downloading a copy first is not built yet. The sheet says so rather than showing a button that would produce nothing. There is also nothing we could send you, for the same reason.

Deleting the app removes everything too. There is a fuller explanation on the deletion page.

A6. What Apple and Google collect on their own#

When you download or update any app, Apple and Google process data under their own privacy policies, not ours. If you have agreed with them to share crash and usage data with developers, they may make aggregate reports available through their consoles. We do not control that, we cannot switch it on or off for you, and you can change it in your device settings. It is not something the app does.

A7. What the store forms say for this version#

For this version the correct answer on Apple’s App Privacy questionnaire and Google Play’s Data Safety form is “Data Not Collected”, with no data used to track you and no data linked to you. The app’s iOS privacy manifest says the same thing, and the two plugins in the build carry their own manifests declaring that they collect nothing either.

A8. Children#

Own Hub is not directed at children. It is a fitness and health tracking tool intended for adults.

Because this version has no account and collects nothing, there is no personal data of anyone, of any age, for us to hold.

A9. Your rights, in this version#

Under Egypt’s Personal Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020 you have the right to know what a company holds about you, to see it, to correct it, to have it deleted, to take it with you, and to object to particular processing.

In this version we hold nothing about you, so there is nothing to see, correct, port or ask us to delete. Everything the app holds is on your phone and under your control, and A5 is how you clear it.

If you want to tell us that, or ask anything else, write to mahmoud.reda.acc@gmail.com.

Part B, when a server is connected#

None of Part B is active. It describes the release in which sign-in, identity verification and cloud storage are switched on. Until that release exists, nothing below happens.

B1. The promises that shape this section#

  1. Your ID photo and your selfie are deleted after the check. Only the pass or fail result is kept. The app shows you the date they were deleted, read from the record itself, not from a sentence someone typed.
  2. There is no AI face or gender classification anywhere in this product. We never guess your sex from a photograph. The check reads the sex digit that the Egyptian government already put in your national ID number.
  3. Your cycle data never goes to analytics, under any circumstance. Neither does your phone number, your name, your verification result, your weight, or any photo.

In this version nothing is kept anywhere but your own phone, for as long as you keep it there. The periods below belong to the release Part B describes, and several of them still have to be set with counsel before any server exists.

ID image and selfie

24 hours, then deleted automatically.
Safety reports

Payment and invoice records

B6. Deleting an account, once accounts exist#

You will be able to delete from inside the app, and to ask us to delete without installing the app at all. The public page at Delete your data explains both.

Three things would survive on purpose, and we would rather say so than let you find out:

  1. A one-way scramble of your national ID number, kept in closed form with the link to you removed, so that someone removed from the app cannot rejoin the next day.
  2. Safety reports made by you or about you, with the account identifiers replaced by a meaningless token.
  3. A record that a deletion happened, reduced to dates and a status.

B7. Security, in plain language#

  • Your account would be protected by a code sent to your phone. There is no password to steal, and we never ask for one.
  • Data would travel over an encrypted connection and be encrypted where it is stored.
  • You could only read your own data. The rules enforcing that live on the server, not in the app, so a modified copy of the app cannot read anyone else’s data.
  • The app cannot mark itself as verified. The verification result is written by our server and by nothing else. That is the single most important rule in this product: a women-only app whose gate can be set by the phone holding it is not a women-only app.
  • Progress photos would be encrypted on your device with a key held in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore.
  • Honest limits: no system is perfectly secure, and a device left unlocked is outside our control.
