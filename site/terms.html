2. What Own Hub is, in this version#

  • A fitness app for women in Egypt. You can follow workouts, log what you train and eat, set a weekly goal, track measurements you enter yourself, and record your menstrual cycle.
  • You need an account, and you are checked once. You sign up with your Egyptian mobile number and a code sent by SMS. There is no password, and there is no profile that anyone else can see.

3. Who may use it, and why it is for women only#

  • Own Hub is for women aged 18 or over who hold an Egyptian national ID card. It is a women-only space for one reason: so that women in Egypt can train, follow their bodies and their cycles, and look after their health somewhere they feel safe and their privacy is protected. This reflects the Constitution’s protection of women, of private life, of health and of everyone’s right to sport (Articles 11, 18, 57, 59 and 84). It is not a judgement about anyone else.
  • You must be a woman aged 18 or over who holds an Egyptian national ID card.
  • If you are 18, 19 or 20: Egyptian civil law gives full contractual capacity at 21. Own Hub is free and asks nothing of you in money, and nothing in these Terms takes away any right the law gives you because of your age.
  • How membership is confirmed: once, when you join. Your sex and age are read from your national ID number. A live selfie is compared with the portrait on your card. If the automatic check cannot decide, or you choose the niqab route, a woman from Own Hub reviews your case and contacts you by phone within 72 hours to finish your check. The photographs of your card and your face are never stored. We never guess your sex or your age from your face.
  • One account per person. You may not use anyone else’s identity documents, or let anyone else use your account.

Read this section even if you skip the rest.

  • Own Hub is a fitness and wellbeing app. It is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, and it is not a substitute for a doctor.
  • Workouts, meal suggestions, calorie and macro figures, and anything the app shows about your cycle are general guidance, not prescriptions. Cycle dates are estimates.
  • Talk to a doctor before you start, especially if any of these apply to you: you are pregnant or have recently given birth; you have a heart, joint, bone or blood-pressure condition, diabetes, or an eating disorder; you are recovering from surgery or an injury; you take medication that affects exercise.
  • Stop exercising if something hurts, and get medical help if you need it. The app never penalises you for stopping or resting.
  • Where the app shows guidance about severe symptoms, it is telling you to seek care, not treating you. In an emergency, contact emergency services or go to a hospital.
