2. What Own Hub is, in this version#

A fitness app for women in Egypt. It lets you follow workouts, log what you train and eat, set a weekly goal, track measurements you enter yourself, and record your menstrual cycle.

You need an account, and you are checked once. You sign up with your Egyptian mobile number and a code sent by SMS. There is no password and no profile that anyone else can see. Your training and meals are kept on your phone and, once you are verified, with your account. Your cycle and health information stays on your phone unless you turn on backup yourself. The Privacy Policy sets out exactly what is kept and where.

It is free, and there is nothing in it to buy. No adverts, no in-app purchases, no subscriptions, and no payment of any kind inside or outside the app.

It has no community and no coaching in this version. Nothing connects you to another person. Section 12 describes what changes if those arrive, and they will arrive only in a later version, with a later version of these Terms.

3. Who may use it#

Own Hub is made for women in Egypt, and that is the product’s purpose.

You must be a woman aged 18 or over who holds an Egyptian national ID card. Membership is confirmed once, when you join, through the Egyptian national ID, which carries sex and date of birth in the ID number itself, and a live selfie that is compared with the portrait on the card. If the automatic check is unsure, or you choose the niqab route, a woman reviewer phones you to arrange a short video check. The photographs of your card and your face are never stored. We never guess your sex or your age from your face.

  • One account per person.

4. Own Hub is not medical advice#

Read this section even if you skip the rest.

  • Own Hub is a fitness and wellbeing app. It is not a medical device, not a diagnosis, and not a substitute for a doctor. Nothing in it is a clinical opinion about you.
  • Workouts, meal suggestions, calorie and macro figures, and anything the app shows about your cycle are general guidance. They are not prescriptions.
  • Talk to a doctor before you start, especially if you are pregnant or recently gave birth, have a heart, joint, bone or blood-pressure condition, diabetes, an eating disorder, are recovering from surgery or injury, or take medication that affects exercise.
  • Stop exercising if something hurts, and get medical help if you need it. Nothing in the app can require you to continue. The app never penalises you for stopping or for resting.
  • Where the app shows guidance about severe symptoms, such as heavy bleeding or severe pain, it is telling you to seek care, not treating you. If you think it is an emergency, contact emergency services or go to a hospital. Do not wait for an app.
  • The app does not know your medical history. It only knows what you typed into it.

5. Allergies and food#

The app carries an allergy guard that refuses to say a dish is safe when it cannot tell. It fails towards refusing rather than towards reassuring, which is the right way round.

You are responsible for checking what you eat. Nothing in the app replaces reading a label or asking whoever cooked it.

6. Your data, and your phone#

Your account is tied to your mobile number. Signing in needs a code sent by SMS to that number, so keep your phone and your SIM card safe.

  • What you log is on your phone. Anyone who can unlock your phone can open the app and read it. If that matters to you, use your phone’s own lock.
  • Your training and meals are also kept with your account once you are verified, so they come back when you sign in on a new phone. Your cycle and health information comes back only if you turned on health backup; otherwise it stays on the old phone.
  • You can delete your account and your data, from Profile, then Your data. Deletion completes on our server, and it cannot be undone.
  • We cannot recover anything after deletion. Once your account is deleted, your logs and your history are gone.
  • Downloading a copy of your data is not built in this version, and the app says so rather than offering a button that produces nothing.

The Privacy Policy explains all of this in more detail.
